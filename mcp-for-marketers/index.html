01 / THE SHORT ANSWER

So, what is an MCP?

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. Think of it as a common set of rules for connecting an AI assistant to other tools.

If the assistant is a new teammate, MCP is the agreed way it can ask the right systems for information or request a task. You still decide which systems it can access and what it is allowed to do.

“

MCP is like a standard electrical outlet. It makes connecting things easier; it does not decide which appliance is safe, who may use it, or whether the toast is any good.

02 / THE LUNCHBOX VERSION

Imagine asking AI this.

“Which webinar leads became opportunities?”
WITHOUT A CONNECTION

It might give you a lovely generic answer. It has no idea what happened in your webinar.

WITH AN APPROVED CONNECTION

It could ask your webinar platform for attendees, ask your CRM for opportunity records, then help you compare them.

The MCP server offers specific abilities to the assistant, such as “look up attendees” or “find opportunities.” The connected application handles the exchange. Your existing systems and their APIs still do the underlying work.

03 / HOW IT WORKS

Four moves. No magic wand.

  1. 01
    You ask a question.

    “What did the campaign contribute to pipeline?”

  2. 02
    The assistant sees its approved abilities.

    One connection might offer campaign records; another might offer CRM data.

  3. 03
    It requests information or an action.

    The connection passes the request to the relevant system and returns a result, subject to the setup and permissions.

  4. 04
    The assistant drafts an answer.

    A marketer checks the definitions, source records, attribution logic, and conclusion before sharing it.

Three things an MCP connection might offer: Tools to do or retrieve something, resources to read, and prompts as reusable ways to ask. What is available depends on the product and your setup. MCP is a standard for the connection, not a magical accuracy upgrade.

04 / THE MARKETING OPPORTUNITY

What could your team do?

Start with a real question and the approved systems that hold the evidence.

01 / ABM

Which accounts are heating up?

Connect: CRM + intent platform + campaign data

Get: A prioritized account review with evidence for each recommendation.

02 / CAMPAIGNS

What should we fix?

Connect: Ad platform + web analytics + CRM

Get: A draft diagnosis tying spend, engagement, conversion, and pipeline together.

03 / FOLLOW-UP

Who attended but never heard from us?

Connect: Webinar platform + marketing automation + CRM

Get: A list to review and a proposed follow-up workflow.

04 / MESSAGING

Did the launch message land?

Connect: Survey or feedback source + call notes + content performance

Get: Themes, supporting evidence, and questions to investigate.

05 / REGIONAL ABM

Can we brief the next account play?

Connect: Approved account data + content library + CRM

Get: A first draft grounded in actual accounts and approved messaging.

These are possible workflows, not promises that every platform has an MCP connection or that all data can be combined automatically. Access, data quality, field mapping, and permissions matter.

05 / KEEP YOUR EYEBROWS ON

Where the BS sneaks in.

An assistant may connect to real data and still produce a bad conclusion. A webinar attendee is not automatically an influenced opportunity. A shared account name is not proof that the same people engaged. A 20% lift without a time window or denominator is a pretty number wearing a fake mustache.

Before you act, ask:

  • Where did this come from? Name the systems, records, time period, and definitions.
  • Could it change something? Start with read-only access; review sends, edits, exports, and budget changes before they happen.
  • Who is allowed to see it? Respect customer data, consent, company policy, and the access limits of each connection.
  • Can we check the math? Match important claims to source records and have a human approve the decision.
THE CMO RULE

Connected AI can help gather evidence and do the busywork. A human still owns the claim, the customer experience, and the decision.

06 / START SMALL

Try one project.

Start with one narrow question, such as “Which webinar attendees from our target accounts need a sales review?” Give the assistant access to only the approved records needed. Have it return account names, matching evidence, missing data, and a proposed action for approval. Compare a sample to the original systems before you trust the whole list.

If the result is useful, expand carefully. If it confidently says every attendee bought something, send it to the BS Detector.
